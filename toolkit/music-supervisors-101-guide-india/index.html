Music Supervisors 101 Guide (India)

A free, practical guide for independent Indian artists. Part of the Indie Music India toolkit.

Who music supervisors are, how they find music for Indian films and OTT series, and how to get your music in front of them.

What Is a Music Supervisor?

A music supervisor is the person responsible for selecting, licensing, and managing all music used in a film, web series, advertisement, or other audiovisual production. They sit between the director's creative vision and the practical realities of rights clearance and budget.

A music supervisor in India typically:

  • Works with the director and editor to identify where music is needed and what type fits the scene
  • Searches for existing music (source music) that matches the creative brief
  • Negotiates sync licences with rights holders (artists, labels, publishers)
  • Manages the music budget for the production
  • Coordinates with the original score composer (a separate role)
  • Ensures all rights are properly cleared before release

Music Supervision in India: How It Actually Works

India's music supervision sector is at a significantly earlier stage than the US or UK. Dedicated, independent music supervisors are rare. Instead, the function is typically performed by:

1. The film's music director / composer: In Bollywood, the music director often oversees all music in the film, including source cues. Original score and song selection are often the same person's responsibility.

2. In-house OTT music teams: Netflix India, Amazon Prime Video India, Disney+ Hotstar, SonyLIV, and Zee5 have in-house music coordinators or managers who handle music licensing for original productions.

3. Production house music teams: Larger production companies like Excel Entertainment, Dharma Productions, or Maddock Films have internal teams that manage music rights for their productions.

4. Independent music supervisors: A small but growing number of independent practitioners work on advertising, branded content, and smaller productions. The Indian Advertising Agency Association (AAAI) is a relevant professional body.

What Kind of Music Do Indian Productions Need?

INDIAN OTT SERIES (Netflix, Amazon, Hotstar originals):

  • Contemporary Indian indie music is increasingly used in youth-oriented series
  • English-language international songs used for aspirational/urban character moments
  • Specific mood/genre requirements vary by production type
  • Hindi film songs sometimes used (heavy rights clearance required)

Bollywood Films

  • Most music is commissioned as original songs from the music director
  • Sync of pre-existing independent tracks is rare in mainstream Bollywood but growing
  • Art-house and parallel cinema more open to licensing independent music

Advertisements and Branded Content

  • The most accessible entry point for sync in India
  • Ad agencies regularly use indie music for brand campaigns
  • Brands like Bacardi, Bira 91, Kingfisher, boAt, CRED, and Red Bull India have used indie music in campaigns
  • Fees range widely: INR 50,000 (small digital campaign) to INR 20,00,000+ (national TVC)

Web Series and Youtube Originals

  • Smaller budgets than OTT, but volume is high
  • Indian YouTube creators and web series producers often approach independent artists directly
  • Fees: typically INR 10,000–1,00,000 per track depending on production size

Documentary Films

  • Often limited budgets, but international documentary exposure can be creatively valuable
  • Many documentary filmmakers will negotiate flat fees for Indian artists

How to Get your Music to Music Supervisors

Method 1: Direct Outreach

Find music supervisors and in-house music coordinators via LinkedIn (search: "music supervisor India", "music licensing India", "sync licensing India"). Also search in credits of OTT shows you admire.

Pitch email format (brief): Subject: [Song Title] — [Genre/Mood] | Sync Available Body: 2 sentences about the song, a streamable link (SoundCloud, private Spotify, Dropbox), and confirmation that sync rights are available and unencumbered. Do not attach audio files.

Method 2: Music Libraries and Pitching Platforms

Upload your music to:

  • Musicbed (musicbed.com): Premium sync library. Curated; application required.
  • Artlist (artlist.io): Global music licensing platform used by many video creators.
  • Epidemic Sound: Large platform; apply as creator.
  • Synchedin: More accessible; lower barrier to entry.
  • Musicbroker.com: India-relevant platform.

Note: Library deals are often non-exclusive, low-fee, and give you broad reach. They are not a substitute for direct sync relationships but can generate passive income.

Method 3: Build Relationships at Industry Events

  • Sundance London / LA (for international supervisors interested in Indian music)
  • IFFI (International Film Festival of India) — Goa: Industry networking opportunities
  • IFFM (Indian Film Festival of Melbourne): Australian-Indian film industry crossover
  • Prism Music Summit, NHMC (NH7 Weekender Music Conference): Indian music industry conferences

Method 4: Music Supervisor Submission Services

Groover (groover.co) and similar platforms allow you to pay a small fee to pitch directly to music supervisors who have opted in to review submissions.

What to Have Ready Before Pitching for Sync

  • High-quality recording (professionally mixed and mastered)
  • Instrumental version available (many sync uses require instrumentals)
  • Rights clearly held: you own both composition AND master, or can license both
  • Clean (radio edit / no explicit content) version available for advertising use
  • Lyric sheet with English translation (if song is in Hindi or other Indian language)
  • Metadata complete: ISRC, composer/lyricist credits, IPRS registration confirmed
  • Quick response time: sync deals move fast. Be reachable within 24 hours.

Sync Licensing Process in India — Step by Step

Step 1: Music supervisor or brand agency contacts you with a brief Step 2: You provide the relevant track(s) for consideration (private stream link) Step 3: If selected, they request a quote (you or your publisher/admin quotes the sync fee) Step 4: Terms negotiated: fee, territory, exclusivity, term, use type Step 5: Sync Licensing Agreement signed (see Sync Licensing Agreement template) Step 6: You deliver the audio file in the specified format (typically WAV or AIFF) Step 7: Payment: typically 50% on signing, 50% on delivery or on first day of broadcast Step 8: Collect TDS certificate if payer is a company (Section 194J for royalty payments)
