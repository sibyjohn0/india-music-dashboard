Radio Stations & Programmers 101 Guide (India)

A free, practical guide for independent Indian artists. Part of the Indie Music India toolkit.

A comprehensive guide to India's radio station ecosystem, how programming decisions are made, and who the key players are in 2026.

India's Radio Industry Overview

India has one of the world's largest radio audiences. The FM sector operates under Ministry of Information & Broadcasting licensing. After Phase III FM licensing, private FM stations now operate in over 300 cities.

Total private FM stations in India (2026): approximately 350+ Key regulatory body: Ministry of Information & Broadcasting (MIB) Collecting royalties for recordings on radio: PPL India Collecting royalties for compositions on radio: IPRS

Major Private FM Networks

RADIO MIRCHI (Times Group / Entertainment Network India Ltd)

  • Station count: 65+ stations across India
  • Focus: Hindi contemporary, Bollywood, mass-appeal pop
  • Key markets: All metro cities + Tier-2 and Tier-3 coverage
  • Typical audience: 22–45, broad socioeconomic spread
  • Programming approach: Centrally managed with some city-specific content
  • Key shows: Morning drive shows are highest-rated. Mirchi Top 20 chart show.
  • Relevance for indie: Very limited unless you have a mainstream Hindi hit or a significant brand deal that includes radio. Some special programs like "Mirchi Studio" feature independent artists.
  • Corporate contact: ENIL (Entertainment Network India Ltd), Times of India Group

BIG FM (Reliance Broadcast Network)

  • Station count: 58+ stations
  • Focus: Hindi pop, Bollywood, youth content
  • Audience: 18–35
  • Programming: Mix of national and local content
  • Big FM Freshers: A segment/show concept for new artists on some stations
  • Relevance for indie: Similar to Mirchi — difficult without mainstream Hindi crossover

Red FM

  • Station count: 45+ stations
  • Focus: Youth, alternative, irreverent commentary, music
  • Audience: 18–30, more urban and educated
  • RED FM is the most indie-friendly of the major private networks
  • RED FM has hosted smaller artist features and show appearances for non-Bollywood acts
  • Relevance for indie: The highest viable target in private FM for English/alternative indie acts

RADIO CITY (Music Broadcast Limited)

  • Station count: 39+ stations
  • Focus: Music-led programming, slightly older demographic (25–45)
  • Radio City is known for music content quality relative to peers
  • City 101.9 Mumbai: Flagship station with some non-Bollywood programming
  • Relevance for indie: Moderate; some specialty programming by city

FEVER FM (HT Media)

  • Station count: Concentrated in metros (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata)
  • Focus: Hindi and English bilingual content, urban elite audience
  • Audience: 22–40, higher income urban
  • Relevance for indie: English-language Indian artists have a slightly better shot here than on Mirchi/Big FM

All India Radio (Air)

AIR is a public broadcaster under Prasar Bharati, India's autonomous public broadcasting organisation.

Key Air Channels for Musicians

Vividh Bharati: National entertainment radio. Hindi film songs, drama, features. Widest geographic reach in India, including rural.

FM Gold (Delhi, Mumbai): Bilingual (Hindi/English). More sophisticated programming. Covers classical, indie, and special music features. More accessible to independent artists than private FM.

FM Rainbow (Multiple cities): Regional language programming. Best channel for regional language independent artists.

National Channel: Classical music focus. Relevant for Hindustani and Carnatic classical artists.

Air Artiste Registration

To receive performance fees for broadcast on AIR, artists must be registered as AIR artistes (Grade A/B/C system). Visit allindiaradio.gov.in for the audition and registration process. Once registered, AIR pays performance fees each time your music is broadcast.

How Programming Decisions Are Made

Music Director Role

At each station (or regional cluster), the Music Director (MD) or Head of Music decides:

  • Which songs enter the active playlist
  • How often each song is rotated (heavy / medium / light)
  • Which artists get interview or show slots

The MD typically:

  • Reviews new submissions from labels and independent artists
  • Monitors chart data and audience research panels
  • Attends label listening sessions for major releases
  • Has autonomy on local programming content

Label Relationships

Major labels (Sony Music India, Universal, Warner) have dedicated label managers who meet radio MDs regularly and bring pre-release content. This is the structural advantage labels have over independent artists.

DIY Path

Independent artists who want radio placement without a major label or plugger must: 1. Build a direct relationship with the station's MD or programming team 2. Attend industry events where radio programming staff are present 3. Engage a radio plugger who has existing MD relationships 4. Submit music via the station's official submission channel (most have one, albeit less prominently advertised)

STATION CONTACT APPROACH (by station type)

Private FM (Major Networks) Best approach: Radio plugger with existing MD relationships. Cold email has low success rate. Backup approach: LinkedIn to programming/music team. Personal introduction via mutual contact.

All India Radio Best approach: Submit via AIR's official artist registration process. Register as an auditioned artiste for paid broadcast rights.

Regional FM Best approach: Directly contact the city station's music team. Regional stations are often more accessible to local artists.

Online / Streaming Radio Best approach: Submit via the platform (Gaana, JioSaavn radio sections) through your distributor. Algorithmic placement is increasingly important.

Radio Royalties: Who Gets What

When your song plays on a radio station in India: 1. The radio station pays a licence fee to PPL India (for the recording) and IPRS (for the composition). 2. PPL India distributes the master recording royalty to whoever owns the recording (you, if independent; your label, if signed). 3. IPRS distributes the composition royalty to registered composers and lyricists. 4. The radio station does NOT pay you directly — all payments go through PPL and IPRS.

This is why IPRS and PPL India registration is essential before pursuing radio.
