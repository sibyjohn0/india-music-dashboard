DIY College Radio Promotion Guide (India)

A free, practical guide for independent Indian artists. Part of the Indie Music India toolkit.

How to promote your music through India's college radio and student media ecosystem — the most accessible grassroots broadcast channel for independent artists.

Why College Radio and Student Media Matter for Indie Artists

India's college and university ecosystem is one of the most active cultural environments in the country. IIT festivals (Mood Indigo, Kshitij), NIT fests, DU fests, and major university cultural festivals draw tens of thousands of students and attract mainstream media attention.

Student radio, student newspapers, and college arts organisations reach your most likely early adopters: young, urban, digitally-native listeners in the 18–24 age bracket who are actively looking for new music and are highly influential within their social networks.

Unlike commercial FM, college media is genuinely open to independent and alternative music. Student programmers are fans first — they respond to great music regardless of label or streaming numbers.

The College Media Landscape in India

Online Student Radio Stations and Podcast Networks

Many Indian colleges have moved from traditional radio to online streaming stations and podcast networks hosted on platforms like SoundCloud, Spotify, YouTube, and Instagram Live.

Examples:

  • Radio IIT (various IIT campuses)
  • Radio Timbre (Symbiosis Pune)
  • Radio Echoes (Christ University Bengaluru)
  • Campus radio initiatives at DU, JNU, BITS Pilani, SRCC, and more

Student Media / Entertainment Clubs

Virtually every major Indian college has a music or entertainment society that:

  • Organises live events and fests
  • Publishes a student newspaper or magazine
  • Runs Instagram and YouTube channels promoting student culture
  • Has booking authority for college fest performances

College Fest Bookers

The cultural festival circuit (Mood Indigo at IIT Bombay, Rendezvous at IIT Delhi, Waves at BITS Pilani, Kshitij at IIT Kharagpur, Saarang at IIT Madras) is the most prestigious and best-funded college performance circuit in India. Headlining fees at top fests can reach INR 5,00,000+ for established acts. Support slots start at INR 15,000–75,000.

Step-by-step: DIY College Radio Promotion

Step 1: Build your Target List

Research college radio stations, media clubs, and festival organisers at colleges in your target cities. Sources:

  • College websites (look for: Cultural Society, Media Society, Fest page, Radio Club)
  • Instagram: Search "[College Name] music" or "[College Name] cultural"
  • LinkedIn: Find cultural fest coordinators or music club heads
  • Reddit: r/IndiaMusicProduction, r/India, city-specific subreddits often discuss college fests

Create a spreadsheet with: College | City | Media Outlet/Club | Contact Name | Instagram | Email | Role | Notes

Step 2: Personalise your Outreach

Student media coordinators respond to personal, specific outreach far better than mass emails.

Template email:

Subject: Music for [Station/Show/Club] — [Artist Name]

Hi [Name],

I'm [your name], a [genre] artist from [city]. I've been following [station/club/fest] for a while — I actually remember when [specific reference to something they've done or a show they ran].

I'd love to get our new track '[Song Title]' on your radar — it's been getting some love from [publication/playlist mention] and I think your listeners would connect with it. Here's a stream link: [link]

Would you be open to playing it on [show name / station]? Or if you're booking acts for [fest name], I'd love to talk about performing.

[Artist name] [Instagram | Spotify | YouTube]

Step 3: Offer Value, Not Just a Request

Things that make a college media contact more likely to respond:

  • Offer an exclusive acoustic session or interview for their audience
  • Offer to participate in their podcast or YouTube channel as a guest
  • Offer tickets to your next show in their city
  • Offer to contribute a guest playlist for their station
  • Be willing to play their college fest for a reasonable fee or even a reduced rate early in your career (the exposure and relationships are worth more)

Step 4: Follow Up and Maintain the Relationship

College media coordinators change every year (students graduate). Maintain relationships by:

  • Sending new music each time you release
  • Attending college fests as an audience member and connecting with media teams
  • Congratulating them on shows or events you notice on Instagram
  • Referring other artists to them when you hear they're looking for acts
