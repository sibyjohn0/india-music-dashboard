Publicists 101 Guide (India)

A free, practical guide for independent Indian artists. Part of the Indie Music India toolkit.

What publicists do, how music PR works in India, when you need one, and how to find and evaluate one.

What Is a Music Publicist?

A publicist (or PR manager) handles an artist's relationship with media — press, digital publications, journalists, bloggers, and podcasters. Their job is to:

  • Get your music covered by credible outlets
  • Manage the narrative around your releases and career
  • Secure interviews, features, and reviews
  • Develop relationships with journalists and editors on your behalf
  • Place you in media moments that build long-term visibility

A publicist is NOT:

  • A social media manager (though some PR firms include this)
  • A booking agent
  • A marketing manager (though there is overlap)

How Music PR Works in India

India's music PR industry is small. There are a handful of established music-specific PR firms in Mumbai, Delhi, and Bengaluru, and a larger number of general entertainment PR agencies who also handle musicians.

The most effective publicists in the Indian indie space are typically:

  • Individuals who have worked at music media outlets and transitioned to PR (they have built-in editorial relationships)
  • Small boutique agencies with specific genre expertise (indie, electronic, hip-hop)
  • Generalist entertainment PR agencies with a dedicated music division

What Good Music PR Looks Like in India

  • A Wild City or Homegrown placement for an artist on their roster
  • A Rolling Stone India feature within 6 months of signing
  • Consistent placement in at least 2–3 credible outlets per release
  • Radio plugging (some PR firms also handle radio — ask upfront)

What Bad Music PR Looks Like

  • Guaranteed press placements in paid editorial sections (advertorial masquerading as editorial)
  • "We'll get you 50 articles!" — volume of low-quality coverage is worth less than 1 genuine Wild City review
  • Agencies with no music-specific media relationships (they know lifestyle/fashion PR but not music)
  • No month-end reporting on outreach and results

PR Fees in India (2026 Benchmarks)

Retainer structure is standard for music PR in India.

Level | Monthly Retainer | What's included ──────|──────────────────|-──────────────── Entry-level / boutique | ₹10,000–25,000/month | 1–3 publications targeted, 1 press release per release Mid-level | ₹25,000–60,000/month | 5–10 publications, radio pitching, interview facilitation Established firm | ₹60,000–1,50,000+/month | Full campaign management, 10+ publications, national press, event PR

Campaign-based (per release, not ongoing): Entry: ₹15,000–40,000 per release campaign Mid-level: ₹40,000–1,00,000 per release campaign

When to Hire PR

  • Before a major release (single, EP, or album) where you want press coverage
  • When playing a major show or festival that deserves coverage
  • When you have a genuinely newsworthy story (award, label signing, international tour)

When Not to Hire PR

  • Before you have any recordings to share — no publicist can generate coverage for an artist with no music
  • When your budget doesn't cover at least 3 months of retainer (1 month of PR rarely generates results)
  • For every small release — save PR budget for your strongest work

Finding a Publicist in India

METHOD 1: Look at music press credits When you read a Wild City or Homegrown feature you admire, the article sometimes credits a PR contact. Note the name and agency.

METHOD 2: Ask artists whose press you respect Artists in your genre who have received consistent quality coverage likely have a publicist. Ask them who they use.

METHOD 3: LinkedIn search Search: "music publicist India", "entertainment PR India", "music PR manager Mumbai/Delhi/Bengaluru"

METHOD 4: Industry WhatsApp groups Music industry groups for Indian indie artists often discuss PR recommendations.

Questions to Ask a Potential Publicist

Before engaging a publicist, ask: 1. Who are 3 current clients in my genre and what coverage have you secured for them in the past 6 months? 2. What specific outlets do you have active relationships with? 3. Can you share examples of recent press placements (Wild City, Homegrown, national press)? 4. What does your monthly reporting look like — what will I receive and when? 5. Do you handle radio as well or only press? 6. What is your process for a new release campaign? 7. What is your notice period if I want to end the engagement? 8. Do you handle social media or is that separate?

Red Flags

  • Cannot name specific recent placements with real outlets
  • Promises guaranteed coverage ("we guarantee Rolling Stone India coverage")
  • Asks for 6-month minimum before showing results
  • No reporting structure mentioned

Doing your Own PR (DIY PR)

If budget does not allow for a professional publicist, many independent artists handle their own press, especially for early releases. This is viable when:

  • You have a genuine story to tell
  • You can write clearly and concisely
  • You are willing to be patient — DIY PR takes longer than professional PR

Key DIY PR steps: 1. Write your own press release (see Media 101 Guide for pitching guidelines) 2. Build your own media contact database 3. Research the publications and what angles they cover 4. Send targeted, personal pitches (not mass BCC emails) 5. Follow up once only 6. Build relationships by attending gigs and industry events where press are present
