The best music distributor for independent artists in India

There is no single right answer, only the one that fits how you release. Here is how to decide.

By Siby John, thirteen years inside the platforms that decide which music gets seen: Marketing Manager at YouTube India from 2022 to 2025, working with 400+ artists and labels, now Senior Creator Manager at LinkedIn. Reviewed 1 August 2026.
Short answer

If you want to publish music in India and get onto the music platforms independent artists in India actually use, Spotify, Apple Music, JioSaavn, YouTube Music, a distributor is how you do it. There is no single best distributor. The right one depends on how often you release, whether you want a flat annual fee or a revenue split, and whether you need India-specific services like caller tunes or sync support. What matters most is simple: it can take a fee or a cut, but never your rights.

What a distributor actually does

A distributor delivers your mastered track, artwork, and metadata to Spotify, Apple Music, JioSaavn, YouTube Music, Instagram, and the rest, and collects your earnings back. You do not need a record label to do this. The differences between distributors come down to pricing, rights, and the extra services they bundle.

Flat fee vs revenue split

This is the core decision:

  • Flat fee (for example a fixed annual price for unlimited releases): best if you release often and earn enough that a percentage would cost you more than the fee. You keep all your royalties.
  • Revenue split (the distributor takes a percentage, often with no upfront cost): best if your volumes are low or uncertain and you would rather not pay in advance.

Do the math on your actual streams. The right model is the cheaper one at your real numbers, not the one with the nicer homepage.

DistroKid vs TuneCore vs others

Rather than crown a winner, compare on model. Flat-fee services suit frequent releasers who want simple wide reach. Per-release or split models can make sense at lower volumes or when you want bundled publishing collection or sync support. Judge each by your release frequency and what you need beyond upload, not by brand name.

India-specific things to check

  • Caller tunes on Airtel and Jio, a real revenue and reach channel here, not every distributor offers it.
  • Reach to JioSaavn and Gaana, confirm delivery to the platforms Indian listeners actually use.
  • Publishing and IPRS, most distributors handle the recording, not your composition royalties. You still register with IPRS yourself.
  • Sync-ready metadata, clean splits, ISRC, and clear ownership, if placements matter to you.

The one rule: keep your rights

Whatever you choose, use a distributor that takes a fee or a share of revenue but never ownership of your master or publishing. Read every agreement for rights-transfer language, and never assign your rights to move music onto platforms. People also ask

Can you change your music distributor without losing streams?

Yes, if you migrate carefully and keep the same ISRCs where possible, so playlists and stream counts carry over. Never take music down without a migration plan.

Do music distributors take your rights?

A good distributor takes a fee or a share of revenue, never ownership of your master or publishing. Read every agreement for rights-transfer language before you sign.

Which distributor is best for caller tunes on Jio and Airtel in India?

Caller tunes need a distributor or aggregator with telco deals, and not all of them offer it, so ask before you distribute if this channel matters to you.

Is DistroKid available in India, and how much does it cost?

Yes, DistroKid works in India. It charges a flat yearly fee (roughly 1,500 to 2,000 rupees for the base plan) and lets you upload unlimited songs while keeping 100% of your royalties, which makes it cheap if you release often.

DistroKid vs CD Baby: which is better for Indian artists?

DistroKid charges a yearly fee and takes no cut, best if you release frequently. CD Baby India charges a one-time fee per release but takes a small commission, better if you release rarely and want a permanent listing. For most active independent artists the flat-fee model wins.

What is the cheapest music distributor in India, and the best for a beginner?

The cheapest routes are free-tier distributors that take a small revenue share instead of an upfront fee. For a beginner who wants no upfront cost and simple setup, a free tier is the easiest start, see our free distribution guide.

Which digital music distribution companies work in India?

The main options for Indian artists are DistroKid, CD Baby, TuneCore, Amuse, RouteNote, and a few India-focused services. All push your music to Spotify, Apple Music, JioSaavn, and YouTube Music; they differ mainly on fee model and extras like caller-tune delivery.

How long does Spotify take to approve music after I distribute it?

Once your distributor delivers the track, Spotify usually processes it in two to five days, though it can take up to a week or two around busy release periods. Deliver at least four weeks early so you can also pitch it for editorial playlists.

How do I distribute a full album in India?

The process is the same as a single: upload the tracks, cover art, and metadata to your distributor, set one release date, and it goes live as an album across platforms. Number the tracks correctly and keep the metadata consistent so they group as one release.

What does Reddit recommend for music distribution in India?

Threads on music subreddits generally favour flat-fee distributors like DistroKid for frequent releasers and warn against services that lock your rights or take large cuts. Treat Reddit as a sentiment check, then verify the fees and terms yourself before choosing.
