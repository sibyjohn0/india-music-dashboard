How to pitch your music to blogs and press in India

Coverage comes from pitching the right person the right way, not from blasting everyone. Here is how to do it well.

By Siby John, thirteen years inside the platforms that decide which music gets seen: Marketing Manager at YouTube India from 2022 to 2025, working with 400+ artists and labels, now Senior Creator Manager at LinkedIn. Reviewed 1 August 2026.
Short answer

Pitch the blogs, writers, and podcasts that actually cover your genre, with a short, specific note giving one clear reason it is worth their readers' time, and an easy streamable link. Relevance and timing beat volume, and one polite follow-up is enough.

Find the right people

Look at who is already writing about music like yours, follow their bylines, and note where they invite pitches. A relevant writer beats a big outlet that never covers your genre. Our Reviewer Database lists 1,100+ blogs, magazines, podcasts, and curators covering Indian indie, with contacts.

Write the pitch

Keep it short: who you are, one clear reason this is worth their readers' time, a streamable link, and your EPK. Lead with the hook or news angle, not your life story. Editors skim, so front-load the point.

Time it right

Give writers lead time before release, and do not mass-blast, a generic email to fifty people gets ignored where a specific one to five gets read. Personal and relevant wins.

Follow up once

One short, polite follow-up after several days, adding any new reason to care, is enough. If there is no reply, move on, relationships are long and pushiness costs you the next release too.

Beyond blogs

Podcasts, campus and online radio, and independent playlist curators are often more open than mainstream press, and all take the same approach. See playlist pitching for the streaming side.

PR is not marketing

PR earns coverage through other people; marketing is what you own and control. You need both. The takeaway for pitching: be specific, be relevant, be brief, and respect a no.

The takeaway

Target the right writers, pitch short and specific with your EPK, time it, and follow up once. The PR chapter of the Playbook goes deeper, and the programme handles pitching for artists through its PR network.
